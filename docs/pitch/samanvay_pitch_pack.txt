Everything you just saw proves the model works. This part answers who
pays for it.

We're not selling to a resident. A resident sees one thing: their
geyser starts forty minutes later, with an override button, same as a
smart plug they already recognize. The complexity lives in a
coordinator a facility manager operates, same as the lift AMC nobody
in the building understands either.

Three buyers, in order. Bulk HT societies first: they already pay a

rupees. Weeks to close. DISCOMs second, and bigger: we pitch deferred
transformer capex, not software. A transformer upgrade costs lakhs, we
defer a share of that for less. The metering this depends on is
already being installed nationally under RDSS, whether we exist or
not. New build developers third, selling EV ready societies as a
launch differentiator.

Put your own numbers in. That's not a projection for the slide. That's

One gap, before you ask: no discom smart meter API, because it doesn't
exist for third parties yet. We don't need it. Every bulk HT society
already sub meters its own flats for billing, that data is ours to
ask for directly. And we only need aggregate power and a few

What's next isn't a bigger model. It's one real society's data sharing

PART 2. WHAT THIS IS, IN 100 WORDS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Samanvay coordinates flexible electricity load across a shared
distribution transformer in an Indian residential society, so sixty
flats can add AC, geysers, and EV charging without tripping
infrastructure sized before any of that existed. Every fifteen
minutes it forecasts uncontrollable load as a distribution, not a
point estimate, computes headroom against the transformer limit using
the ninety fifth percentile forecast, and allocates that headroom
fairly across households, tracked with a fairness ledger so no single
flat is deferred every month. Dispatch is deterministic and
auditable, never a black box or a learned policy. The first paying
customer is the facility manager, not the resident.


PART 3. COMMERCIAL AND BUSINESS QUESTIONS JUDGES WILL ASK
----------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Won't this be too complex for a normal resident to use?
No. The resident's entire interface is a notification with an
override button, the same pattern as a smart plug they already own.
The optimizer, the forecaster, and the fairness ledger are all
operated by the facility manager, exactly like the lift AMC or the
security contract, which no resident understands either and nobody
asks them to.

2. Is this actually scalable?
Yes, by design. Household response is local and rule based, so the
central coordinator only optimizes allocation, not every device. That
means going from sixty flats to six hundred, or from one society to a
hundred societies on one discom feeder, is a deployment and data
pipeline question, not an algorithmic one.

3. Will government or private organisations actually buy this?
Three buyers, in order of how fast you can close them. Bulk HT
societies first, through their existing facility management company,
weeks to months, small deal size. DISCOMs second, pitched as deferred
transformer capex rather than software, twelve to twenty four months,
much larger contract value, and this is the one that makes it a
company rather than a feature. New build developers third, as an EV
ready differentiator at launch, converting to a recurring subscription
after handover.

4. Who is the first paying customer, specifically?
A bulk HT society's facility management company. They already sell an
O and M subscription for lifts, pumps, and security. This is a new
line item on an existing invoice, not a new vendor relationship to
build from nothing.

5. What tool or platform is this built on?
Python for the forecaster and optimizer, LightGBM for the quantile
models, a MILP solver for the allocation problem, DuckDB over Parquet
for storage because the workload is append once and read many, and
Three.js for the 3D society view with procedural geometry only, no
imported assets. Edge compute runs the coordinator locally so a
transformer decision never depends on an internet connection.

6. Where does the data come from, if not a live discom feed?
Real published research datasets for training: IIT Bombay IRED for
household load shapes, Low Carbon London for price response behaviour,
a Hyderabad AC dataset for thermal modelling, iAWE for appliance
signatures, I-BLEND for common area load, and Open-Meteo for weather.
All historical, all named, none of it claimed as real time.

7. Will you use the discom's smart meter API?
Not yet, and we say so directly. Discom smart meters exist at scale,
over seven crore installed, but there is no standard third party
access API for that data today. We don't build the product around a
dependency we don't control.

8. Then how do you get live operational data in a real deployment?
Two paths. First, every bulk HT society already sub meters each flat
for its own internal billing. That data belongs to the society, not
the discom, so getting access is a direct commercial conversation with
the RWA or the facility manager, no regulator involved. Second, for
societies without submetering, a low cost retrofit: a current
transformer clamp plus a small microcontroller publishing over MQTT,
the same proven pattern behind consumer energy monitors sold
worldwide.

9. Isn't building your own metering hardware a huge undertaking?
No, because the system does not need smart meter grade data to
function. It needs aggregate active power per flat at fifteen minute
resolution, plus a handful of availability flags for flexible
devices. The rich, expensive, high resolution data is only used once,
offline, to train the appliance and archetype models. That's a
deliberate design choice to keep live deployment cheap, not a
limitation we're working around.

10. What already exists in this market, and why hasn't someone done
this?
Smart metering vendors instrument the meter but don't coordinate load.
Commercial ESCOs sell demand charge reduction to single large
industrial consumers, not distributed residential coordination.
International utility side platforms do something closer to this, but
aren't localized to Indian tariffs, appliance mix, or Indian society
governance. We're not claiming an empty market, we're claiming a
specific, undeveloped wedge inside an adjacent one.

11. What is actually defensible about this, technically or otherwise?
Deterministic, auditable dispatch, and a fairness ledger. No learned
policy or black box model decides whose water heater waits, which
matters because a system that saves money by always deferring the
same six flats gets the vendor voted out at the next general body
meeting. Nobody adjacent is selling explainability and fairness as
the product.

12. What's the revenue model?
A performance fee as a share of verified demand charge savings for
bulk HT societies, or a flat monthly subscription per flat where a
performance fee isn't practical. For DISCOMs, positioned around
deferred capital expenditure rather than a software licence, priced
against the capex it avoids.

13. Is this a venture scale business or a small services business?
Told plainly. The bulk HT society segment alone is a real,
bootstrappable subscription business, but modest revenue per customer
on its own. The DISCOM segment is where a venture scale outcome lives,
and it depends entirely on landing one credible, published pilot with
real breach and capex deferral numbers, then using that as the
reference sale for the next twenty DISCOMs.

14. What could actually kill this?
Discom data access remaining closed longer than expected, if the
submeter and retrofit paths can't cover enough of the addressable
market on their own. Said directly rather than glossed over, because
you cannot coordinate what you cannot measure.

15. What's the single next step that matters most right now?
Not a bigger model. One real society's or facility management
company's data sharing agreement, using their own historical
submeter billing data to validate the forecaster on top of the
published research datasets. That's the proof point that answers "is
this real" better than any dataset citation, and it's the same thing
an early investor would ask for first.


PART 4. PRODUCT AND SIMULATION QUESTIONS JUDGES MAY ALSO ASK
----------------------------------------------------------------------

16. What problem is this actually solving?
A shared transformer sized before EV charging existed now sees
everyone's AC, geyser, and charger overlap at seven in the evening.
Nobody is wasting anything, the coincident load simply exceeds what
the transformer was built for.

17. Is the demand data real or synthetic?
Synthetic for the world simulation, built bottom up from real named
research datasets and archetype behaviour, not measured from any one
real building, and that distinction is stated openly rather than
implied.

18. How does a flat's AC actually decide to turn on?
A real hysteresis thermostat gated by occupancy: if someone is home
and indoor temperature exceeds the comfort ceiling, the AC switches
on, and that draw feeds directly into the same physical thermal
equation that updates indoor temperature, so temperature is a real
consequence, not a display number.

19. What happens when the transformer overloads?
It actually sheds load. Non critical common infrastructure like
streetlights and the water pump gets cut first, then a rotating
subset of flexible household load, prioritized so it isn't the same
households every time, and every shed decision is recorded, not
painted onto the display afterward.

20. What happens during a full grid outage?
Grid import goes to zero. Security and fire systems are assumed
battery backed and stay on. Lifts stop, since they have no backup and
shouldn't run without power regardless.

21. If you run the same scenario twice, is the result identical?
Yes. Every random element, from which archetype a flat gets to
weather and occupancy noise, is drawn from a seeded generator, so the
same seed reproduces the same output exactly.

22. Where is the actual optimization happening, is anything AI here?
The world simulation itself runs uncontrolled, deliberately, so there
is a real baseline for a controller to improve against. The
optimization is the separate quantile forecaster and chance
constrained allocator, which substitutes the ninety fifth percentile
of the demand forecast for the mean in the capacity constraint, one
line of code, and that substitution is the entire safety guarantee.

23. Why not reinforcement learning?
Across CityLearn 2021 through 2023, the benchmark competition for
exactly this problem, no top performing team used reinforcement
learning for scheduling. Winners used forecasting plus classical
optimization, which is the shape we follow, and we cite the result
rather than apologise for the choice.

24. How do you know households aren't all peaking at the same time?
Measured directly as a coincidence factor, peak of the combined load
divided by the sum of each household's individual peak. It should
land between 0.4 and 0.6 for a realistic population; if it's near
1.0, the generator is broken and everything built on it is invalid,
so it's checked early, not assumed.

25. What assumptions are hand picked versus measured?
Every appliance rating, thermal parameter, and comfort band is a
plausible, stated assumption, not measured data, because no dataset of
that granularity exists to measure from. Labelled as such throughout,
not implied to be more authoritative than it is.

26. What's the fairness mechanism, specifically?
A running ledger per household of cumulative kilowatt hours deferred
and comfort deviation, reported as Jain's fairness index plus the
worst case household's deviation, with headroom allocated to equalise
that ledger over time rather than just minimising total cost.

27. Does the household get a real say, or is this forced?
It's a quota signal, not a command. A resident can override at any
time, and the system is explicitly tested holding the transformer
limit with ten to twenty percent of households overriding, so it
degrades gracefully rather than assuming perfect compliance.

28. What happens to solar power at night?
Nothing is hardcoded to zero. Output is calculated from real sun
position astronomy, and it becomes zero as a direct consequence of the
sun being below the horizon, the same calculation that turns
streetlights on and off.

29. Is the whole system explainable, or is there a black box anywhere?
No learned model or LLM sits in the dispatch path. The optimizer is a
deterministic, auditable calculation, because a decision about whose
water heater waits has to be explainable line by line, not inferred
from a black box.

30. What would you fix first with more time?
Real operational data from one actual society to replace the research
dataset training entirely, and the two remaining world scales, colony
and cross society connection, which extend the same simulation to
multiple transformers and shared generation rather than one society
in isolation.
