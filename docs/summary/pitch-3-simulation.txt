SAMANVAY — PITCH 3 OF 3
The simulation: what happens when the day goes wrong
======================================================================


THE ONE SENTENCE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

A controller that works on a normal Tuesday is not a product. The
simulator exists to run the days that break it — a heatwave, half a
feeder going blind, the intake tripping at half past seven — and to
show what the controller does when it can no longer trust its own
inputs.


TWO SIMULATORS, DIFFERENT JOBS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

1. THE BUILDING SIMULATOR — evidence

   Real metered data, real thermal physics, real tariff. Six
   controllers run against four scenarios: 24 complete month-long
   runs at fifteen minute resolution.

   This is where the numbers in Pitch 1 come from.

2. THE SOCIETY SIMULATOR — the demo

   Sixty flats, one shared connection, an estate you can watch hour by
   hour. Five stress scenarios on the keys 1 to 5. This is the thing a
   judge actually looks at.

They share the tariff, the constraint registry and the forecaster
interface. They are not the same simulation and we do not present them
as one.


THE SIX CONTROLLERS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

  no_control           A deadband thermostat at a fixed 24 °C, water
                       heater always hot, EVs charging flat out on
                       arrival. Price-blind and peak-blind — what an
                       uninstrumented building does.

  rule_based           Time-of-day rules. What a competent facility
                       manager already does by hand.

  mpc_mean             Our controller with the mean substituted for
                       the q95. Same optimiser, same physics, same
                       seed. The ONLY difference is the quantile.

  ours                 Chance-constrained on the calibrated q95.

  mpc_oracle_rolling   Perfect forecast, rolling horizon.

  oracle               Knows the whole month in advance. Not a
                       controller you can build — the ceiling on what
                       any controller could do.

mpc_mean is the important one. It isolates the single claim of this
project: not "MPC is good", but "the quantile you put in the capacity
constraint is what decides whether you breach". Everything else is
held identical so that difference cannot be attributed to anything
else.


THE FOUR BUILDING SCENARIOS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

  none             A normal month. The baseline.
  heatwave         Cooling load that does not switch off.
  sensor_dropout   The meters stop reporting mid-run.
  outage           Supply is lost and critical load moves to backup.

sensor_dropout is the one worth demonstrating. A forecaster running on
a stale estimate should not keep claiming the same confidence. Ours
widens its band when its inputs degrade, so the controller
automatically becomes more conservative without anybody intervening.
A model that keeps quoting a narrow interval after its data has gone
is not being brave, it is being wrong.


THE FIVE SOCIETY SCENARIOS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

  1  Normal day      A weekday in the shoulder season.

  2  Heatwave        38 °C at four in the afternoon. Every AC in the
                     society running and none of them stopping.
                     Forecast band widens 1.25x.

  3  Sensor drop     Submeters stop reporting at 16:00 — most of an
                     entire feeder. The forecast is running on a stale
                     estimate AND KNOWS IT: the band widens 2.6x and
                     the controller gives back headroom rather than
                     pretending.

  4  Grid outage     Intake trips at 19:30. The estate goes dark and
                     critical services move to backup — lifts, fire
                     panel, pump house, emergency lighting. Lifts,
                     water and fire are never shed. That is a hard
                     constraint, not a preference.

  5  Festival        Deepavali eve. Lights on every balcony, guests in
                     every flat, every geyser in the estate. Twelve
                     same-evening requests arrive at once.

Scenario 5 is the demo moment. Twelve requests collapse into one
grouped card, one button staggers them into two groups, ₹3,891 becomes
₹0, and Saturday settles at 90.8 kVA. Nobody is refused.


WHAT THE SIMULATION IS HONEST ABOUT
----------------------------------------------------------------------

DETERMINISM. Everything is generated from a seeded load shape plus the
tariff. The renderer computes nothing — it reads a day and draws it.
Run it twice, get the same month. A demo that reshuffles on every
reload is a demo you cannot be questioned about.

THE GHOST. Every chart carries the schedule the society used before,
drawn alongside the one it uses now. The saving is always stated
against a real alternative, never against zero.

THE FLOOR. Critical load has a floor the optimiser cannot go under. No
flat can be deferred more than the cap in the constraints file, and a
fairness ledger tracks who has been asked so the same flat is not
deferred every month. Fairness is measured with a Jain index and
reported per feeder and per block, not just as a single society
number that can hide a badly-treated corner.

THE OPT-OUTS. Some flats have opted out entirely and some meters are
offline. Both are in the simulation by default, because a controller
that only works when every flat participates and every meter reports
is not a controller for a real building.


WHAT IT IS NOT
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The society simulation is a simulation. The thermal physics and the
tariff are real, the load shapes are calibrated against real
residential patterns, and the household archetypes are drawn to sum
correctly — but sixty real flats have not run this.

The building results ARE on real metered data, held out, walk-forward.
Those two claims stay separate in every document we produce.


HOW TO DRIVE IT IN A DEMO
----------------------------------------------------------------------

  Keys 1–5     switch scenario
  Space        play the day
  Q W E R T Y  switch tabs
  G            swap the scene treatment

  1. Start on NOW, normal day. Sixty homes, live.
  2. Press 2. Watch the band widen and the headroom shrink.
  3. Press 3. Point at the band widening again — the model knows its
     inputs went stale.
  4. Go to COMPARE. Drag the split: 87.0 kVA forecast-only against
     84.4 with this system.
  5. Go to EVIDENCE. The calibration plot, the month carpet, the
     rendered invoice, the fairness bars.
  6. Press 5, go to REQUESTS, and stagger the festival.

Six steps, about ninety seconds, and every number on screen came out
of the same seeded model.
