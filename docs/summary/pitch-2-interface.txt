SAMANVAY — PITCH 2 OF 3
The interface: two ends of one set of facts
======================================================================


THE ONE SENTENCE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

There are two apps — an operator console and a resident app — and they
are not two apps. They are one set of facts seen from two ends, which
is why the operator can never see a number the resident has not agreed
to show them.


WHY THE INTERFACE IS NOT DECORATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

A demand-control system fails for social reasons long before it fails
for technical ones. The optimiser can be correct and the product can
still die, because:

  - a resident does not believe you are only touching the geyser
  - a resident thinks their neighbour is getting a better deal
  - an operator cannot answer "why was my flat asked?"
  - one flat is deferred every single month and nobody notices

None of those are model problems. All of them are interface problems.
So the interface carries the trust, and the rules are enforced in code
rather than written in a policy document nobody reads.


THE RESIDENT APP
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Five screens, one flat. What a flat owner sees, and nothing else.

  MY FLAT      what was moved today, and what it saved
  SUGGESTIONS  things worth doing, each with a price and a cost
  SAVINGS      where the money came from, and what is still unclaimed
  SOLAR        society solar today, and the case for their own panels
  REQUESTS     ask for something; control what we can see

THE HERO IS THE THESIS. The society is billed on its single highest
fifteen minutes of the month, so the app opens on the resident's real
day drawn as ninety-six intervals. The 6–10pm window is tinted amber,
because that is where a unit costs 26 percent more. One interval is
drawn as a hatched ghost, with the filled tick beside it showing where
it went.

That single figure says the entire product: we touched one of your
ninety-six intervals, by twenty minutes, and it saved you ₹4.

EDITORIAL RULES, HELD THROUGHOUT

  - Every number is a rupee amount or a count of minutes. Never kWh
    where money would do.
  - Every automated action states its comfort cost BEFORE its saving.
  - Every decline, toggle-off and pause is stated as free of penalty,
    in the same place as the action.
  - The boundary — lights, fans, TV, cooking, fridge — is repeated on
    the home screen and again inside the consent tier.
  - Contribution is participation, never consumption, and the formula
    is published on the same screen as the rank.

CONSENT IS A LADDER, NOT A SWITCH

Four tiers, and the system works fully at tier 1 for everyone:

  0  The society meter          always on, not a consent decision
  1  Your flat counts in it     always on, never shown alone
  2  Your flat's meter          optional — buys you your own figure
  3  Appliance readings         optional — buys better timing

Higher tiers buy better results, never basic function. Turn tier 2 off
and your savings show as the society average instead of your own
number, you stop being ranked, and nothing else changes. The
degradation is honest and it is visible at the moment of choosing.

DIGNITY, ENFORCED IN CODE

The medical emergency button protects the flat immediately, tells
management, and removes that flat from ranking. The operator has no
control to override it. That is not a policy, it is an absent button.


THE OPERATOR CONSOLE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Six tabs. NOW, TIMELINE, COMPARE, EVIDENCE, REQUESTS, LOG.

THE REQUESTS TAB IS THE DEEPEST THING IN THE PRODUCT.

Three sections, in this order:

  A  Needs your decision      typically zero to three cards
  B  Approved automatically   31 of 34 this month. Deliberately dull.
  C  Coming up                seven days, granted requests stacked
                              over the forecast peak

Section B should feel boring. Boring is the point: it is the section
that proves the system is working without demanding attention. A queue
that is always full stops being read, so anything fitting inside the
forecast headroom is approved before the operator ever sees it.

THE CLUSTER CARD IS THE ONE TO DEMO

Two flats want the same Saturday evening. Approved alone, each costs
the society ₹0 and ₹1,276. Approved together they cross the ceiling
and cost ₹3,890.

Deciding them one at a time produces two numbers that both go wrong
the moment either is approved. So they are not two decisions. They are
one card, showing all three figures and the sentence explaining why
the first two mislead — and one button that staggers them into two
groups, landing the evening at 90.8 kVA for nothing.

Nobody is refused. Both flats get their evening; one starts two hours
later than it asked.

NO CONFIRMATION DIALOGUES ANYWHERE

Every decision commits at once and carries a ten-second draining undo.
After that window it is final. An operator making thirty decisions an
hour does not read a modal, they dismiss it.

WHAT THE OPERATOR IS NEVER SHOWN

The flexibility budget shows the distribution — how many flats have
used 0, 1–3, 4–5, or all 6 of their boost days. It never lists which
flats. Auto-declined requests appear behind a toggle rather than being
hidden, because automation you cannot see is automation you cannot
answer for.


THE LINK BETWEEN THEM — DEMO THIS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The two apps share one store. Open them side by side and:

  1. Send a request from the resident app. It arrives at the top of
     the operator's decision queue as a real card with its own cost
     arithmetic, instantly.

  2. Accept a suggestion. The resident's headline climbs, the
     operator's live feed gains a row reading ₹38/mo, and the badge
     decrements — in the same frame, no refresh.

  3. Raise the medical flag. A red banner appears on the operator's
     queue and they have no way to override it.

  4. Withdraw tier 2. The resident's figure drops to the society
     average and the operator's consent coverage falls 47 → 46.

THE LINE TO SAY WHILE DOING IT: "These aren't two apps talking to each
other. It's one set of facts with two ends."


DESIGN
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The ground is the cool pale slate of a meter cabinet, not paper,
because this is a civic utility and not a consumer finance app.

One accent. Amber means the hours between 6 and 10 in the evening,
when a unit costs 26 percent more, and it is allowed to mean nothing
else. Slate means society solar. Green means money saved. Red appears
exactly once in the entire app, on the medical button.

Money is set in Anek Latin — drawn in India by Ek Type — narrowed on
its width axis so a large rupee figure is not a loud one. Meter
readings, interval times and clock labels are set in mono, because
machine data should never read as prose.

No chart library anywhere. Six figures in CSS and inline SVG.

Responsive to mobile, keyboard-navigable, visible focus, reduced
motion respected. The ninety-six ticks draw themselves left to right
once, and then hold still. That is the only orchestrated motion in the
app.
