03 · said before anyone asks

Where the data actually comes from

Not the discom's smart meter API. It doesn't have a public interface for third parties yet, and we say that plainly rather than let a judge catch it.

Path A · now

Society‑owned submeters

Every bulk HT society already sub‑meters each flat to generate its own internal bill. That data belongs to the society, not the discom. Getting access is a contract with the RWA or FM company, not a regulatory filing.

Path C · fallback

Retrofit CT clamp + ESP32

For transformer‑constrained societies with no submetering. A non‑invasive current clamp plus a ~$5 microcontroller, publishing over MQTT — the same pattern behind Emporia Vue and Sense. Proven engineering, not a research problem.

Path B · future

Discom smart meters

7.24 crore installed and growing under RDSS. No standard third‑party access API exists yet. We're positioned to converge on it once it does — not betting the product on it arriving.

We don't need smart‑meter‑grade data. The coordinator runs on aggregate active power per flat at 15‑minute resolution, plus a handful of availability flags for flexible devices. High‑resolution, appliance‑level data is used once, offline, to train the archetype models — it never has to be collected live again. That's a design choice, not a limitation.

05 · the highest‑leverage next step

What we're asking for

Not a bigger model. One real data‑sharing agreement.

01

One society or FM company's submeter data, shared with permission

Six to twelve months of real flat‑wise billing data, validated against our forecaster on top of the published research datasets. This answers "is this real" better than any dataset citation can, because it's our own pilot, not someone else's study — and it's the same proof point an investor asks for first, because it's the leading indicator that a real sale is close behind it.
